# Segmentation and ICP fit

The output is one beachhead segment, named specifically enough that you could list actual
companies in it, plus the reasoning for choosing it over the runners-up.

## Segment by trigger and behaviour, not size

"Mid-market companies in India" is not a segment. It shares no buying behaviour, no trigger,
no budget structure. Useful segments are defined by combinations of:

- **Observable firmographics** — sector, regulatory regime, business model, tech stack,
  operating footprint. Observable matters: a segment you cannot enumerate is one you cannot
  sell to.
- **A trigger event** — what makes the problem urgent this quarter rather than someday. A
  funding round, a regulation, an audit, a migration, a breach, a departure, crossing a
  scale threshold. Segments without triggers produce long, stalling sales cycles.
- **Current behaviour** — what they do about the problem today, which determines what you
  displace and what you must be cheaper than.

## Map the buying committee

For each candidate segment, identify separately:

| Role | Question |
|---|---|
| Pain owner | Who suffers daily |
| Economic buyer | Who releases budget |
| Technical evaluator | Who can block on security, architecture, compliance |
| Champion | Who benefits from the change being seen to happen |
| Veto | Who can stop it for reasons unrelated to merit |

Where the pain owner and economic buyer are the same person, the sale is dramatically
shorter. Where they are three levels apart, note it as a structural cost — it changes the
required price point, because a cheap product cannot fund a long enterprise sale.

## Fit scoring

Score candidates, showing the evidence behind each score rather than the score alone:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Pain intensity | Are they already spending on this |
| Budget authority | Can the pain owner authorise, or must they escalate |
| Reachability | Is there a channel to them that does not require a field sales team |
| Time to value | Can they see benefit in days, or does it need migration |
| Willingness to switch | What is the cost of leaving what they use now |
| Expansion potential | Does landing one team open the rest |
| Reference value | Does winning them make the next ten easier |

Reachability and time to value are the ones founders systematically under-weight. A segment
with intense pain and no efficient channel will consume the company.

## Beachhead selection

Pick for evidence generation and compounding, not size. The right first segment is small
enough to serve well, homogeneous enough that one product satisfies all of them, reachable
without a sales organisation, and adjacent to a larger segment that watches it.

State explicitly what the beachhead is a beachhead *to*. A segment with no adjacency is a
niche, which can be a fine business but is a different plan and should be labelled as one.

## Anti-patterns

**Segment as persona.** Invented individuals with names and frustrations. These cannot be
counted, found or sold to.

**Segment by size alone.** Company size predicts budget process, not need.

**The composite everyone.** A segment defined so broadly that every finding applies to it
and none distinguishes it.

**Choosing the segment with the most pain.** Usually also the one with the most entrenched
incumbent, the longest procurement cycle, and the highest switching cost.

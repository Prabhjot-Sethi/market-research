# VC pairing

Match the thesis to investors by what they have actually funded recently, not by what their
site says they focus on. Stated theses are marketing and lag the portfolio by a year or more.

This analysis assesses fit. It does not assemble contact details or outreach targets from
personal data — the output is a reasoned shortlist with the basis for each entry.

## Per firm

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Stage and typical check | Determines whether this round is even in scope |
| Last 18 months of relevant investments | The real thesis |
| Geography and whether they lead there | Many funds follow but do not lead outside a home market |
| Partner with the domain | Funds do not invest, partners do |
| Portfolio conflicts | A competing portfolio company usually ends the conversation |
| Follow-on behaviour | Whether they reserve and participate later |
| Board posture | Involved, hands-off, operationally interventionist |
| Recent fund vintage | A fund near the end of its deployment window behaves differently from one at the start |

## Read the portfolio, not the thesis page

Three tests:

1. **Adjacency.** Have they funded companies selling to the same buyer? A fund with four
   portfolio companies selling to hospital procurement understands that buyer regardless of
   category.
2. **Pattern.** Do they repeatedly fund a shape — infrastructure, marketplaces, PLG,
   regulated markets — that your thesis matches or contradicts?
3. **Timing.** Did the relevant investments cluster in one period and then stop? That
   suggests the thesis was tried and is now closed, which is a finding.

## Signal from absence

A fund conspicuously absent from a category everyone else funded either has a considered
reason or missed it. Both are worth knowing. If a partner has publicly explained a pass on
the category, that is among the most valuable evidence available on the thesis itself — it
is a sophisticated argument against, made by someone with no reason to flatter.

Feed those arguments into the consumption-validation and competitive analyses. They often
name the failure mode before you find it yourself.

## Conflicts

Flag explicitly. A fund with a direct competitor in portfolio is not a prospect, and time
spent there leaks information. Distinguish direct competitors from adjacent portfolio
companies, which can be an advantage.

## Warm paths

Where a route exists through the user's own network, note that a route exists and through
which relationship or company. Do not compile personal details on individuals; the useful
output is "a path exists via X" so the user can act on their own relationships.

## Output shape

A ranked shortlist. Per firm: why they fit this specific thesis, the evidence IDs, the
partner with the domain, conflicts, and the honest objection they are most likely to raise.

That last column is the most useful part of this analysis, and the reason to do it before
fundraising rather than during. The objections are largely predictable from the portfolio,
and they are the same objections the business itself must answer.
